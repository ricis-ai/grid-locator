How grid squares work

A grid square is a short code for a patch of the Earth. Hams use it to say where they are without giving their home address, and to collect squares for awards and contests.

The code has 4 or 6 characters. The first two are letters. They name a big field, 20 degrees wide and 10 degrees tall. The next two are numbers. They name a square inside that field, 2 degrees wide and 1 degree tall, about 168 kilometers wide and 111 kilometers tall near Chicago.

The last two letters, when there are 6 characters, name a small square inside that square. Near Chicago a small square is about 7 kilometers wide and 5 kilometers tall. Squares get narrower toward the poles, because the lines of longitude meet there, so the same square is wider near the equator and narrower up north.

Take EN61CN, the square over Tinley Park, Illinois. E N is the field. 6 1 is the square inside it. C N is the small square. The centre of that small square is at 41.5625 degrees north, 87.7917 degrees west (minus 87.7917), and the nearest named place to that centre is Tinley Park itself.

Grid squares are also called Maidenhead locators, after the town in England where the system was agreed in 1980.

About the data

United States places come from the United States Census Bureau Gazetteer, 2025 edition, public domain. It lists 32,350 places: every incorporated town and city and every census-designated place in the United States.

Places outside the United States come from GeoNames, used under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license. This page lists 7,887 of them: cities of 75,000 people or more, plus every national capital.

Three limits to know. First, outside the United States only larger cities are listed, so in a rural area abroad the nearest named place may be a long way off. The page always says how far it is. Second, a town position is a single centre point from the source data, not the town border, so a place next to a square may show as a few kilometers away. Third, in the ocean or near the poles the nearest named place can be very far away indeed.

The nearest place is found by measuring from the centre of the square to every place in the list and taking the closest one. The town may not be inside the square. The page says the distance and direction from the centre so that is always clear.

Nothing you type leaves your phone or computer. The page loads its place list once from this same site, and every lookup after that happens on your own device.
